# Evidence, People Analytics, and Quantification

## 1. Evidence ledger

Maintain:

Claim | Type | Evidence | Source | Confidence | Implication

Type must be one of:
- case fact,
- external fact,
- inference,
- assumption,
- hypothesis.

Confidence:
High / Medium / Low.

Important recommendations should not rest silently on low-confidence claims.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy

Prefer:
1. supplied case evidence,
2. supplied primary research,
3. company/official sources,
4. peer-reviewed research and meta-analysis,
5. government/institutional data,
6. reputable consulting and industry benchmarks,
7. practitioner examples.

Avoid citation theater:
ten weak sources do not equal one strong source.

## 3. Benchmark transferability

For every benchmark ask:

Population comparable?
Industry comparable?
Geography comparable?
Job type comparable?
Time period current?
Metric definition comparable?
Intervention comparable?
Causal or merely correlational?

If transferability is weak, label it directional.

## 4. Quantification hierarchy

Use the strongest available level:

Level 1 — Activity
participants, completions, usage.

Level 2 — Behavior
manager actions, applications, internal moves, interview quality.

Level 3 — People outcome
retention, productivity, mobility, quality of hire, absence.

Level 4 — Operational outcome
capacity, cycle time, error rate, service level, project delivery.

Level 5 — Business outcome
cost, revenue, margin, risk, customer outcome.

Do not claim Level 5 impact from Level 1 evidence without a defensible bridge.

## 5. Value-driver equations

### Attrition

Avoided exits
= eligible population
× baseline regrettable attrition
× expected relative reduction.

Value
= avoided exits
× replacement/productivity cost per exit.

Net value
= value - program cost.

### Hiring

Potential value may include:
- vacancy days reduced,
- agency cost reduced,
- recruiter effort reduced,
- bad-hire rate reduced,
- time-to-productivity improved,
- offer acceptance increased.

### Internal mobility

Potential value:
internal moves
× difference between external and internal fill cost

plus:
vacancy days avoided
× value of capacity per day

plus:
avoidable exits reduced
× cost per exit.

Avoid double counting.

### Productivity

Incremental capacity
= affected FTE
× addressable time
× realistic productivity improvement.

Monetize only when the business logic supports it.

## 6. Scenario model

Use:

Downside:
conservative adoption and effect.

Base:
best-supported assumptions.

Upside:
plausible but not guaranteed.

Show which assumptions drive the spread.

## 7. Break-even

Ask:

How many exits must be avoided?
How many vacancy days?
How much productivity gain?
How many successful internal moves?

Break-even often persuades judges more than a false point-estimate ROI.

## 8. People analytics caution

Correlation is not causation.

Watch for:
- selection bias,
- survivorship bias,
- small samples,
- Simpson's paradox,
- confounding by role/location/manager,
- inconsistent metric definitions,
- historical changes,
- missing data.

For predictive HR use cases include:
- fairness review,
- privacy,
- explainability,
- human oversight,
- monitoring for drift.

## 9. Measurement architecture

Use four levels.

### Leading
Adoption and behavior.

### Outcome
People result.

### Business
Economic/operational result.

### Guardrail
Unintended harm.

Example:

Leading:
% managers completing structured talent conversations.

Outcome:
internal application and move rate.

Business:
critical-role vacancy days.

Guardrail:
adverse-impact ratio or employee trust score.

## 10. Research output

For each research finding report:

Finding:
Evidence strength:
Context:
Relevance to case:
What it supports:
What it does NOT prove.

This prevents research from becoming decorative.
