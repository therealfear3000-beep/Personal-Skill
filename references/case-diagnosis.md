# Case Diagnosis Playbook

## 1. Start with the decision

A case is not a topic. It is a decision.

Write:

Decision maker:
Decision:
Business objective:
People objective:
Target population:
Time horizon:
Constraints:
Success metrics:

If the decision is unclear, infer cautiously and label the inference.

## 2. Separate symptoms from causes

Example:

Symptom:
High regrettable attrition.

Possible proximal causes:
- poor manager experience,
- pay compression,
- weak internal mobility,
- job mismatch,
- burnout,
- location constraints.

Possible system causes:
- manager incentives,
- staffing model,
- workforce planning,
- promotion architecture,
- role design,
- hiring process,
- information asymmetry.

Do not recommend retention initiatives until the relevant causal branch is supported.

## 3. Build a causal chain

Use:

Business outcome
→ people outcome
→ behavior
→ employee/manager experience
→ organizational mechanism
→ root cause.

Example:

Lost delivery capacity
→ critical-engineer attrition
→ employees accept external offers
→ perceived career stagnation
→ internal opportunities are opaque
→ roles are staffed through manager networks
→ mobility market failure.

This diagnosis suggests a different solution than a generic engagement program.

## 4. Use MECE lenses carefully

Potential lenses:

### Workforce

- composition
- cost
- location
- critical roles

### Employee lifecycle
- attract

- develop
- perform

- retain

### Behavioral system
- information
- capability

- incentives
- friction
- social norms
- manager behavior
- process
- structure

### Operating model
- strategy
- governance
- roles
- processes
- technology

Choose the lens that explains the case.
Do not stack frameworks for decoration.

## 5. Segment aggressively when useful

Averages often destroy insight.

Test whether the problem differs by:
- tenure,
- performance,
- critical skill,
- level,
- manager,
- business unit,
- geography,
- workforce type,
- hiring source,
- employee lifecycle stage.

A strong insight often has the form:

"Overall X looks acceptable, but segment Y experiences Z, which drives disproportionate business impact."

## 6. Prioritize root causes

Score each suspected cause:

Evidence: 1–5
Impact: 1–5
Addressability: 1–5

Priority = Evidence × Impact × Addressability.

Do not over-optimize the arithmetic.
Use it to force explicit reasoning.

## 7. Write insight statements

Weak:
"Employees want career growth."

Strong:
"Exit risk concentrates among high-performing employees 18–36 months into role, while internal vacancies remain invisible until formally posted; the issue is not lack of roles but lack of early mobility visibility."

An insight must:
- be specific,
- be consequential,
- explain something,
- change the solution.

## 8. Hypothesis ledger

For each important hypothesis capture:

Hypothesis:
Why it matters:
Supporting evidence:
Contradicting evidence:
Evidence still needed:
Decision if true:
Decision if false:

## 9. Search for contradictions

Look for facts that do not fit the obvious story.

Examples:
- high engagement + high attrition,
- competitive pay + low offer acceptance,
- extensive training + low mobility,
- strong hiring volume + persistent vacancies,
- high program participation + no outcome change.

Contradictions often reveal the real mechanism.

## 10. Final diagnosis output

End diagnosis with:

### What is happening?
Observable pattern.

### Why is it happening?
Supported causal explanation.

### Why does it matter?
Business consequence.

### Where should we intervene?
Highest-leverage controllable mechanism.

### What must the solution NOT do?
Design constraints derived from evidence.
