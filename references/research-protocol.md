# Research and Source Verification Protocol

## Purpose

Use research to sharpen diagnosis, calibrate design, and defend mechanisms.
Do not use research as decoration.

## 1. Start from a decision question

Bad research question:
"What are employee retention best practices?"

Better:
"What evidence exists that internal mobility visibility, manager quality, or location fit predicts early-tenure exits in comparable workforces, and which mechanisms have shown measurable impact?"

Research should test a decision, not collect trivia.

## 2. Use a claim map

Before searching, list the claims the deck may need:

- diagnosis claim,
- causal mechanism claim,
- benchmark claim,
- intervention efficacy claim,
- adoption claim,
- cost assumption,
- business-value assumption,
- risk/guardrail claim.

Mark each:
Must verify / Useful / Optional.

## 3. Source quality

Prefer sources with:
- transparent methodology,
- recent publication date where recency matters,
- comparable population,
- clear metric definitions,
- traceable original evidence.

Use secondary consulting surveys as directional evidence unless methodology supports stronger inference.

Do not cite a press release as causal evidence.

## 4. Primary research

When using interviews or surveys:

Report:
- sample size,
- respondent profile,
- recruitment method if known,
- question wording if consequential,
- whether results are directional or representative,
- possible sample bias.

Small samples can reveal mechanisms and language.
They usually should not be presented as population estimates.

Use direct quotes sparingly and only when genuine.

## 5. Correlations

Before presenting a correlation:
- check sample size,
- verify variable definitions,
- check for outliers,
- avoid implying causation,
- state when analysis is exploratory.

If a correlation comes from a tiny convenience sample, use it to generate a hypothesis, not "prove" the strategy.

## 7. Contradictory evidence

Actively search for evidence that challenges the favored solution.

Record:
- contradictory finding,
- why it differs,
- whether it changes the recommendation.

A case answer becomes stronger when it survives disconfirmation.

## 8. Citation discipline

Place citations near the claim they support.

Do not cite one source at the bottom of a slide for six unrelated claims.

If evidence is weak, say:
"directional"
"illustrative"
"scenario assumption"
"requires validation in pilot"

rather than masking uncertainty.
